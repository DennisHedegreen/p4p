Customer Surfaces

Real public shop surfaces for testing the whole pizzeria lane: menu, order, and public order-status routes.

Customer Fixtures

HTTP-driven synthetic customer actors. They hit the real menu, order, and status endpoints.

Step 1 · Name the fixture

Give the test a readable local identity. This stays in local lab state and makes later flow-building easier.

Step 2 · Choose the target lane

Pick the node this synthetic customer should hit and which public menu surface key the fixture conceptually belongs to.

Step 3 · Describe the customer

This is the human-facing order actor: who is ordering, how they want the order, and any short note.

Step 4 · Build the order

Add the menu items the synthetic customer should place. Keep it small and legible; the goal is test clarity, not cart complexity.

Step 5 · Define the expected result

Say what counts as success. You can keep polling on for a richer status truth or turn it off for fast order smoke tests.

Flow Scenarios

Composed local test flows: infrastructure + node profiles + synthetic customer runs + explicit checks.

Step 1 · Name the flow

Treat a flow as a saved local test recipe. Give it a stable id and a human title so you can reuse it later.

Step 2 · Choose infrastructure

Pick the local services the flow should ensure are running before anything else happens.

Step 3 · Choose node targets

Pick the actual restaurant lanes this flow depends on. These can be shipped profiles or generated node instances.

Step 4 · Choose customer runs

Attach the customer fixtures this flow should perform. You can override the target node per run if needed.

Step 5 · Define checks

Checks are the explicit local success contract for the flow. Refresh the suggested checks or keep your own manual truth.

Step 6 · Keep local notes

Use notes for the human meaning of the flow: what it is proving, what you expect to learn, or what might break.
